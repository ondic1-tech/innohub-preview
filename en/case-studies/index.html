PSP Technologies

Plasma Shock Peening · PSP SPACE

Metal surface treatments for space applications

PSP Technologies is developing Plasma Shock Peening to improve the durability of metal components. Through the PSP SPACE project, we supported its adaptation for space applications, from robotic automation of the equipment and materials testing to laying the groundwork for cooperation with the space industry.

Supported through ESA Technology Broker

▶Find out more

A terrestrial technology in a new domain

Plasma Shock Peening uses a shock wave to induce compressive residual stresses in a metal’s surface layer. The PSP SPACE project focused on adapting this process for space applications to improve component durability and explore opportunities for lighter structures.

Adaptation and validation

Support from the ESA Technology Broker programme helped establish how the terrestrial technology could meet the space industry’s requirements. The project delivered a working prototype for integration with a robotic arm, improvements to the control and optical positioning systems, and materials tests on an aluminium alloy and stainless steel.

Connecting with the space industry

We helped the team present its technology and build contacts within the European space ecosystem. This provided a technical basis for further validation and the preparatory work needed for qualification in specific applications. Development was also supported by ESA Spark Funding.

Market potential analysis

Institute of Molecular Genetics of the CAS

Biotechnology · A new generation of snake antivenoms

From research to international applications

For the Institute of Molecular Genetics of the CAS, we analysed the market potential of a new generation of snake antivenoms based on IgY antibodies. The study identified priority markets in sub-Saharan Africa and proposed potential partnerships and next steps towards practical applications of the research.

Market analysis and commercialisation support

▶Find out more

A new generation of snake antivenoms

The Institute of Molecular Genetics of the CAS is researching antivenoms based on avian IgY antibodies. The assignment was to assess the technology’s potential future applications while bringing together research, commercial and humanitarian objectives.

From a market overview to specific priorities

We compared the needs of the target regions, competing products, pricing conditions and barriers to entry. The analysis identified Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana and Mozambique as promising countries for further validation and highlighted the importance of working with local partners.

Evidence to support further decisions

The study sets out commercialisation scenarios, a preliminary economic model and recommendations for partnerships, funding and pilot validation. Together, these give the team a framework for planning its next steps while taking account of the technology’s early stage of development.
